# Research

I'm a student researcher in the **BRAT Lab** at Wellesley College, where I've spent the past year studying how digital interactive technology can support communication and trust between patients and clinicians — and, more broadly, how thoughtful design can help advance healthcare equity.

---

## How the direction evolved

When I joined the lab, our research focused on how digital interactions could strengthen the patient–clinician relationship in reproductive health — building trust in health technologies and helping address inequities in care.

That focus grew through the research itself. Early conversations pointed us toward the **perinatal (pregnancy-related) experience**, where information overload, high-stakes decisions, and equity gaps are especially pronounced. We broadened our scope accordingly — from a narrower reproductive-health condition to the wider question of how people seek information and make decisions throughout pregnancy.

This is one of the things I value most about research: the direction is shaped by what you learn, not just by what you planned.

---

## Perinatal information-seeking & decision-making

Our work asks how digital tools can help patients navigate pregnancy-related decisions — and how those tools should adapt as a patient's needs change over time.

Two research questions anchor the project:

1. How should a digital interactive system adapt its **information, format, and decision support** as patients' needs evolve throughout pregnancy?
2. Can **context-adaptive systems** improve patients' awareness, agency, and communication with clinicians, compared to static information delivery?

To ground the work in real experiences, our team partnered with reproductive-healthcare community organizations and engaged directly with patients and clinicians through interviews and co-design workshops — mapping when people seek information, which decisions matter most across pregnancy and labor, and where access to good information breaks down.

---

## What we're learning

At a high level, a few themes have emerged from this preliminary work:

- **Timing matters.** Information needs are not constant — they spike at particular moments in the pregnancy journey, which means a tool serving everyone the same static content will miss most people most of the time.
- **Agency is as important as information.** Patients don't just need facts; they need clarity about *which decisions are theirs to make*, and support in communicating their preferences and values to their care providers.
- **Decision-making is non-linear.** People often don't know a choice exists, or don't feel empowered to make one, and their information-seeking loops rather than following a straight path.

Together these point toward **context-adaptive systems** — tools that adjust what they show, how they show it, and how they support decisions as a patient's situation changes, rather than delivering one-size-fits-all information.

---

## Why it matters & where it's going

This preliminary groundwork is laying the foundation for a longer-term investigation into context-adaptive interactive systems for high-stakes healthcare decision-making. Our next steps focus on iterative prototyping and user testing, working toward a design framework that can generalize beyond any single tool — and we're continuing to share this research through academic poster sessions.
