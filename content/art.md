# Studio Art

Work in mixed media, sculpture, and installation — mostly about bodies, systems, and the things that resist being organized. My studio practice and my design practice feed each other: both start from close observation, and both care about how a person moves through something.

## Cyclical
**[CONFIRM TITLE]** · Gouache, magazine paper, and cotton · Spring 2026

![A fallen tree trunk covered with small humanized figures, with new seedlings growing beside the decaying wood and a layered magazine-paper canopy above](/assets/art/artproject1/project-1-final.jpg)

This work explores my emotional experience of PMS and the way the same feelings return each month. A fallen tree trunk is covered with small humanized figures embodying frustration, emotional extremes, and physical pain, while new seedlings grow beside the decaying wood — breakdown and regeneration as parts of one recurring biological rhythm. Fungi, plants, and blackberry-like forms reference medication and personal coping rituals. A layered magazine-paper canopy adds dimension while echoing circular forms throughout the composition, reinforcing the idea of cyclicality.

## Symbiosis
**[CONFIRM TITLE]** · Copper wire crochet, beads, and air-dry clay · Spring 2026
*Co-created with Isabel Li*

![A three-dimensional copper wire and clay structure displayed upside down, with a lower board tracing scent molecules and an upper section tracing a dog's movement path](/assets/art/artproject2/project-2-final-1.jpg)

This piece explores interdependence between humans and animals, specifically how seizure dogs assist people with epilepsy by sensing early signs of an oncoming episode and helping them prepare and respond. Copper wire crochet with beads and air-dry clay build a three-dimensional interconnected system.

The work is displayed upside down. The lower board visualizes scent molecules dispersing from the human body through the surrounding space; the upper section traces the dog's movement path as it detects those signals and responds, guiding itself toward essential items like medication, water, and blankets. The aim is to make a less commonly represented form of human–animal interdependence visible, and to raise awareness of service animals.

![Detail view of the copper wire crochet structure and clay components](/assets/art/artproject2/project-2-final-2.jpg)

## Nothing Stays Sorted
**[CONFIRM TITLE]** · Air-dry clay, wood, thread, wire, and cardboard painted with gouache · Spring 2026 · Interactive installation

![An interactive installation resembling a puzzle game, with black-and-white patterned clay pieces, bendable thread and wire forms, and printed wood squares inside a painted cardboard container](/assets/art/artproject3/project-3-final-1.jpg)

A reflection on — and critique of — my own habit of keeping every thought and feeling organized, avoiding chaos, and resisting experiences that refuse easy categorization. The piece is interactive and deliberately playful, mimicking a puzzle game so the audience tries to organize the components and gradually discovers that nothing can be perfectly fixed or contained.

Three component types fill a painted cardboard container: black-and-white clay pieces with varied patterns and textures, triangular organic forms in thread and wire, and wood squares printed with puzzle-like patterns that have no actual solution. Components can be placed at random, and everything except the clay bends and shifts shape freely. The palette — black, white, red, yellow, and green in varying tints and saturations — is drawn from the colors common to children's toys.

![Detail of the individual components and their varied textures](/assets/art/artproject3/project-3-final-2.jpg)

## Color the Classics
**[CONFIRM TITLE]** · Layered cut paper with slotted wooden standing frame · Fall 2025

![Layered flat paper cutouts forming a carving-like three-dimensional composition in cobalt blue, amber orange, and alabaster white, held in a slotted wooden frame](/assets/art/artproject4/project-4-final-1.jpg)

An exploration of how 2D texture and color can produce a sense of depth — flat paper cutouts layered into a carving-like 3D work. The piece demonstrates cutting precision, figure–ground relationships, texture creation, and the intentional use of hue and saturation.

Textures drawn from Chinese porcelain and pottery are paired with a traditional palette: cobalt blue, cerulean blue, cornflower blue, amber orange, maize yellow, rusty red, and alabaster white. A slotted wooden standing frame holds the boards and allows their order to be rearranged, so foreground, mid-ground, and background can be recomposed in multiple combinations.

![Alternate arrangement of the boards in the slotted frame, showing a different depth composition](/assets/art/artproject4/project-4-final-2.jpg)
